**STATUS 2026-09-14: DRAFT — machine-drafted, persona-reviewed, revised against three critique rounds and three live fact-checks; not yet read by Meng. Citations checked live on 2026-09-14. Two NEEDS MENG markers stand, in notes 3 and 15; voice pass 2026-09-14.**

In 2016 six students sat Singapore's O-Level examinations with help. Accomplices at a tuition center had registered as private candidates. The questions were streamed out of the hall by video, and the answers came back through hidden phones, wireless receivers and earpieces, until invigilators caught one of the students. The center's principal, Poh Yuan Nie, and a teacher, Poh Min Fiona, were convicted of abetting the cheating by conspiracy and lost their appeals in the High Court. Then they obtained leave to refer a question of law of public interest to the Court of Appeal, a procedure that is not an appeal: the court answers the question, and the convictions follow the answer. They did not dispute the facts. They argued that what they had done was, as a matter of law, not cheating.[^1]

The court answered them on 21 November 2022 with a man who does not exist. Consider, it said at paragraph 28, a candidate who submits a forged university degree and wins an unpaid internship on it. He receives experience and nothing else. No property moves anywhere in the story. On the applicants' reading of the Penal Code that is not cheating either, and the forger walks.[^1]

Here is the reading. Section 415 makes two things cheating: deceiving someone into handing over property, and deceiving someone into an act or omission that harms them in body, mind, reputation or property. The charges were laid under the second limb. The examination board had been deceived into accepting the scripts, and the harm alleged was to its reputation. Explanation 1 to the section, one of the clarifying notes the Code prints under a section, adds that "a dishonest concealment of facts is a deception within the meaning of this section," and section 24 defines "dishonestly" as acting with the intention of causing wrongful gain or wrongful loss, both of property. Nothing concealed from the board was aimed at anyone's property. So, the applicants said, no concealment was "dishonest" in the defined sense, so there was no deception, so there was no cheating. Substitute the definition, simplify, walk free.[^1]

Oliver Wendell Holmes described the person who reads a statute that way in "The Path of the Law," in 1897. "If you want to know the law and nothing else, you must look at it as a bad man, who cares only for the material consequences which such knowledge enables him to predict, not as a good one, who finds his reasons for conduct, whether inside the law or outside of it, in the vaguer sanctions of conscience."[^2] A programmer reads that as a threat model. The adversary takes the text as given and searches it for an input the drafter did not anticipate. Security has two names for the person who runs that search, depending on what they do with the result: a black hat uses the input, and a white hat writes it up and sends it to whoever owns the code.[^9] The applicants used theirs.

## The court's four moves

The court's answer has four moves. First, it wrote down every reading "dishonest" in Explanation 1 could bear, and found four: the applicants', on which only a concealment aimed at property counts; that of Benny Tan, whom the court had appointed as independent counsel, on which other concealments count too; the prosecution's, the ordinary meaning of the word; and the court's own, on which "dishonest" names the intention to deceive and nothing more. Second, it followed the applicants' reading to its consequence, that a concealment offense under the second limb could never be made out without a transfer of property, called that "an absurd state of affairs," and produced the forger to show it. Third, it measured the damage to the section: the second limb, which needs no property, would be cut down to cases of property damage. Fourth, it turned on Tan's reading and rejected that too, because it would render Explanation 1 "otiose," a clause that does no work. The answer to the question was no, and the convictions stood.[^1] Which of the four could a program have done? The second and the third, once a person had done the first, and the fourth's test for a clause that does no work. "Absurd" is a judgment about what the section is for, and no solver has one.

Writing down every reading a provision can bear is Layman Allen's program, opened in the Yale Law Journal in 1957 and taught with Rudy Engholm as normalized legal drafting in 1978.[^8] An essay in our repository takes the judgment the rest of the way into a compiler's vocabulary. Section 415 becomes eleven Boolean atoms, each a phrase of the section: "by deceiving any person," "dishonest concealment of facts," "intent to cause wrongful property gain or loss," and eight more. The four readings become a four-way enumerated type, and the section is evaluated under each.[^3] Three encodings of those atoms sit beside the essay, all written by hand after the judgment was read: a model in L4, the language our group at Singapore Management University has been building for six years; a script for Z3, a solver; and a script for Espresso, a minimizer built to shrink circuits. None is generated from another. They share one modeler and one reading of the section, so a mistake in the eleven atoms would survive all three agreeing.

We ran all three again for this draft. Between them they take a fraction of a second. Nobody timed the reading and the writing that came first.[^3] Before any tool is named, the three show this: in every case with no property in it, put a concealment in or take it out and the verdict under the applicants' reading is the same. Wherever property is absent, Explanation 1 is dead text. The L4 evaluator convicts the forger under three readings and acquits him under the applicants'. Z3 proves that no case convicts under the applicants' reading and acquits under the court's, and that across every no-property fact pattern the concealment atom is irrelevant under theirs. Its last query asks for a case the two readings classify differently, with no property in it, and that restriction is the modeler's. The solver returned four atoms set true: concealment, inducement, harm, and harm to mind, one of the four kinds of harm the section lists. That is a class of cases. The forger is one member of it.[^3]

Espresso takes a Boolean function and returns a short sum of cases that computes it, short rather than shortest, because it is a heuristic. The script runs a second minimizer beside it and checks that the two agree on which variables survive.[^3] In the no-property world under the applicants' reading, the offense comes back as four cases, and concealment appears in none of them. Under the court's reading it comes back as eight, and concealment is in four. Law has a name for what the minimizer just did. The presumption against surplusage is the canon that a legislature wastes no words, so a reading that leaves a clause idle is presumptively wrong. The court used it in this case against Tan's reading, and argued against the applicants from reach and absurdity instead. The minimizer says the canon bites their reading too. Dead-code elimination and the canon are one computation put to opposite uses: the minimizer deletes the clause, and the court deletes the reading that killed it.[^3]

One level down, in grammar, the division is the same. Maine's drafting manual tells drafters to omit the serial comma, which left "packing for shipment or distribution" in the state's overtime law readable as one activity or as two. In 2017 the First Circuit held the clause ambiguous, chose the parse by a canon that reads the state's wage laws toward their remedial purpose, and sent the case back.[^4] A parser counts derivations. The choice between them was a canon's.

Parliament had already patched the definition before the question reached the Court of Appeal, for reasons of its own. The Criminal Law Reform Act 2019, in force from 1 January 2020, gave section 24 a second limb, so that "dishonestly" now also covers an act that is "dishonest by the ordinary standards of reasonable and honest persons" and that the actor knows to be so, with no property in it. A new section 6A applies the Code's definitions Code-wide and expressly leaves out "dishonestly" and "fraudulently." Neither was in force in 2016, so neither decided the case. Of the first the court said the amendment "was made out of an abundance of caution and was not intended to change the law"; of the second, that it "clarifies the underlying legislative intent."[^1] That is what a drafter does with a report rather than an exploit: close the reading before anyone has stood on it in court. It is also why the canon against surplusage is only a presumption. Legislatures do spend words on what was already so.

## What the search costs

The people who have run this kind of search longest on systems with money in them work at Amazon Web Services (AWS). Its engineers began writing specifications in 2011.[^11] They wrote them in TLA+, Leslie Lamport's language for a system's states and steps.[^13] Chris Newcombe's 2014 report on it describes an author who spent two weeks learning the language and writing a specification of an algorithm with a known bug, one that had survived design reviews, code reviews and months of testing. The model checker found it in seconds.[^11] Seven years later a twelve-author paper on ShardStore, an S3 storage node, put the cost of its checking harnesses at two formal-methods experts for nine months and a third for three, after which the node's own engineers took them over.[^12]

Jim Woodcock and Peter Gorm Larsen read that public record for Formal Aspects of Computing in June 2026, and we read their seventy pages for the number that would say what the search costs a team that is not Amazon. It is not there. The case studies are success stories, with "little systematic data about neutral or unsuccessful adoption attempts," no controlled comparison with fuzzing, static analysis or code review, and "limited systematic evidence about long-term maintenance effort." Their test for a practitioner: formal methods are "most compelling when design-level defects are expensive, when systems are highly concurrent or fault-tolerant, and when teams can sustain the modelling and maintenance effort alongside conventional assurance."[^14] On the night of 19 October 2025 two copies of a DNS automation in AWS's Northern Virginia region raced, one applying a stale plan over a newer one and the other deleting it as old, and DynamoDB's regional endpoint stopped resolving until the next afternoon. Neither AWS's postmortem nor the TLA+ model a researcher wrote from it in November says whether that automation was ever specified.[^16] The model is a retrodiction, built after the postmortem said what the race was. So are the section 415 encodings.

## Law against the test

A lease with a cure period and a notice deadline, a statute with a clock on a duty: each is a set of parties acting on their own schedules, against deadlines, with nobody sequencing them, and at least one of them reading the text the way the Bad Man reads it. That is a distributed system's shape, and the same search carries over. Enumerate the orders in which the steps can happen. Look for a state where two obligations collide. Post 1 of this series described a statutory obligation with that defect in it. As I recall the fragment, an organization that has found a data breach must notify the affected people and the regulator at once, and the regulator may later direct it not to notify the people. On the page that is a duty and a later override. Run with a clock, the only path that satisfies both — notify the regulator and stall on the people until a direction arrives — breaches the immediacy duty in the interim. A static read misses that. A timed model finds it. We built the model by hand in 2022, in the timed-automata checker from Uppsala and Aalborg (UPPAAL), from the fragment as we understood it. The state we told it to look for — two duties that cannot both be done — was ours to name.[^5] Contracts have been model-checked for that shape since Aspassia Daskalopulu in 2000, whose paper gave "Can the contract ever be in a position in which one party must perform two conflicting actions?" as the kind of question a checker can answer.[^7]

The next step does not carry over. A machine's race is real the moment it runs. A contract's is a hypothesis about the text until a lawyer has checked how the encoding treats provisos and priorities, and whether the doctrine already absorbs it: a later instruction overriding an earlier duty, waiver, estoppel. Law is defeasible. Sarah Lawsky argued in 2017 that at least some statutory reasoning yields conclusions later information can defeat, and post 1 says as much of its own finding: the checker alone did not persuade us the conflict was in the text; the guidance beside the Act did.[^10] In June 2016 whoever drained The DAO, a decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) on Ethereum, wrote an open letter saying they had used "this explicitly coded feature as per the smart contract terms." There the code was the performance, and the loophole was realized the moment it ran.[^7] Ours is a hypothesis about a text a court will read.

Woodcock and Larsen's survivorship point applies to us before it applies to Amazon. Every result in this series is a hit. Of the five pilot engagements post 1 records, three involved a search of this kind, and all three found something: three for three, on a sample nobody chose at random, and the count is our own impression rather than a ledger. The section 415 encodings reproduced a decided case from a reading built after the court had written its hypothetical. That validates the encoding against a known answer and says nothing about whether the search would have found the forger first. The orders a checker enumerates are the ones the encoding permits, and an encoding is itself a reading, so a reported conflict is relative to a reading and never of the text as such. Nothing counts the conflicts that turned out to be artifacts of ours. Nothing counts the encodings that found nothing, either. The group that typed the British Nationality Act into Prolog in 1983 found fewer ambiguities than they expected, and that is probably the common case.[^8] Whether a team can sustain the model as the statute is amended and the contract redlined, Woodcock and Larsen's third condition, nobody has measured for Amazon, and nobody has measured for us. Four case studies and no denominator is the shape of evidence we just criticized.[^15]

Large language models (LLMs) are arriving at the same division of labor from the other side, under a June 2026 slogan borrowed from mathematical AI: "LLM proposes, verifier disposes."[^17] Our division of labor is the section 415 model's. A person proposes the readings and the suspicious hypothetical; the solver runs the search; between them sits a typed encoding whose atoms are the statute's own phrases. Here is the hinge of the case, the ten lines that say when a concealment counts as a deception:[^3]

```l4
GIVEN s IS A `Cheating Scenario`
      i IS AN Interpretation
GIVETH A BOOLEAN
`dishonest concealment counts as a deception` s i MEANS
    CONSIDER i
    WHEN `Second Interpretation` THEN
            s's `dishonest concealment of facts`
        AND `the s 24 requirement is met` s
    OTHERWISE
        s's `dishonest concealment of facts`
```

Two branches for four readings. On every reading but the applicants', a dishonest concealment is a deception in the ordinary sense; only theirs imports the section 24 property gate, which is why the evaluator returns true, false, true, true for the forger. Whether a lawyer can read that beside Explanation 1 is for a lawyer to say.[^17]

A reader can run that model and its two scripts today.[^3] A lease has defects of another kind: a defined term, "the Premises," that appears once as "the Property"; a cross-reference to a schedule the last redline deleted; a rent-review formula that adds a percentage to a sum of dollars. Encoded, the first two are unbound names, which L4's type checker refuses today with "I could not find a definition for the identifier." The third is a units error, and L4 has no notion of units, so the formula passes.[^6] Two of three, this year. The checker that would find post 1's double bind in a rule set is a specification with no prover. The cap-table question — whether one party can force a bad outcome whatever the others do — wants a game solver nobody has built.[^18] The academic version of this argument is a working paper aimed at ProLaLa, the workshop on programming languages and the law that meets alongside the Principles of Programming Languages (POPL) conference.[^19] Two later posts are drafted and not yet out: one on what the compiler checks, which is where the lease belongs, and one on the tests a contract under negotiation ships without.

Holmes insisted that the distinction he was drawing was a practical one: "a bad man has as much reason as a good one for wishing to avoid an encounter with the public force."[^2] The applicants read section 415 the way he said to, found the input, and used it. Run the same search from the drafter's chair and it produces a report instead: wherever property is absent, Explanation 1 is dead text, so tie "dishonest" to something other than property. That is a white hat's deliverable. It is what the eleven atoms, the four readings and the forger's line amount to when they run on a laptop in a fraction of a second. Parliament wrote its own version of the patch in 2019, for its own reasons, before the question reached the Court of Appeal. The court then answered the applicants with a candidate holding a forged degree, an unpaid internship, and no property anywhere in the story. Their reading would have let him walk.
