Regulation Crowdfunding has been amended four times in ways that change an
answer, and every dollar figure above is really a figure _as at a date_.

- **[2016-05-16](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L143 "verbatim")** —
  commencement. Before this date there is no Regulation Crowdfunding to ask
  about.
- **[2017-04-12](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L118 "verbatim")** — an
  inflation adjustment.
- **[2021-03-15](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L122 "verbatim")** — the
  substantive amendment. The offering ceiling rose to
  [$5,000,000](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L151); the investment limit
  changed shape from _lesser_ of income and net worth to _greater_ of; and
  accredited investors were carved out of the cap entirely.
- **[2022-09-20](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L126 "verbatim")** — a
  further inflation adjustment, which moved the cut point, the floor and all
  three financial-statement ceilings. It deliberately left the offering ceiling
  alone, on the Commission's view that the increase the previous year had
  already more than accounted for inflation.

**Why this is not a footnote.** An inflation adjustment sounds like a rounding
exercise. Consider an investor with income of
[$110,000](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L1154) and net worth of
[$115,000](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L1154). Before the last
adjustment both figures cleared the cut point, so the second limb applied and
their cap was [$11,500](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L1162). After it,
income sits _below_ the raised cut point, so the first limb applies and their
cap is [$5,750](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L1163).

Raising a threshold halved that person's capacity. Both answers are correct for
their date, and no "last updated" stamp on a web page can express that.
