Rule 204 is short and severe. An issuer relying on this exemption **may not
advertise the terms of the offering**, except through a notice that does no more
than direct investors to the intermediary's platform.

The notice may carry three things and nothing else: a statement that the issuer
is conducting an offering under section 4(a)(6), with the intermediary's name
and a link to its platform; the terms of the offering; and factual information
about the issuer's legal identity and business location — name, address, phone
number, website, an email address for a representative, and a brief description
of the business.

That is a **closed list**. Compliance is not "did you say the permitted things"
but "did you say the permitted things _and nothing beyond them_".

"Terms of the offering" is itself defined, in an instruction to the section: the
amount of securities offered, their nature and price, the closing date of the
offering period, the planned use of proceeds, and the issuer's progress towards
its funding target.

There is no time limit anywhere in Rule 204. The prohibition simply holds.
