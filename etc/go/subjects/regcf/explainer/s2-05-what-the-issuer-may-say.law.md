Rule 204 is short and severe. An issuer relying on this exemption **may not
advertise the terms of the offering**, except through a notice that does no more
than direct investors to the intermediary's platform.

The notice may carry three things and nothing else. First:

> [A statement that the issuer is conducting an offering pursuant to section 4(a)(6) of the Securities Act, the name of the intermediary through which the offering is being conducted, and information (including a link in any written communications) directing the potential investor to the intermediary's platform](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L604 "verbatim")

Second:

> [The terms of the offering](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L605 "verbatim")

Third:

> [Factual information about the legal identity and business location of the issuer, limited to the name of the issuer of the security, the address, phone number and Web site of the issuer, the email address of a representative of the issuer and a brief description of the business of the issuer](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L606 "verbatim")

That is a **closed list** — the rule's own qualifier is that a conforming notice

> [includes no more than the following information](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L595-L596 "verbatim")

Compliance is therefore not "did you say the permitted things" but "did you say
the permitted things _and nothing beyond them_", which is a much harder test to
satisfy by accident and a much easier one to fail.

"Terms of the offering" is itself defined, in an instruction to the section:

> [the amount of securities offered, the nature of the securities, the price of the securities, the closing date of the offering period, the planned use of proceeds and the issuer's progress toward meeting its funding target](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L612 "verbatim")

**There is no time limit anywhere in Rule 204.** That is a claim about an
absence, so it is worth showing the search rather than asserting it. The corpus
records, at the site, that the section

> [contains no temporal limit at all — its only temporal word is "period", inside the phrase "the closing date of the offering period" in the 204(e) definition, which bounds nothing](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L619-L621 "verbatim")

The prohibition simply holds, for as long as the exemption is being relied on.
That negative fact turns out to matter more than anything positive in this
section, for the reason the encoding note gives next.
