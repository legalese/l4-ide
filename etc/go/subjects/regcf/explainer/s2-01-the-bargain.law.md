Rule 100(a) sets out what an issuer must satisfy to rely on the exemption. Read
plainly, there are five conditions:

1. **Be an eligible company.** Rule 100(b) disqualifies six kinds of issuer.
2. **Do not raise more than the ceiling in any rolling twelve months** —
   currently [$5,000,000](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L151), counting
   only prior sales made in reliance on section 4(a)(6).
3. **Respect each investor's personal cap.** Rule 100(a)(2) is a formula, not a
   table, and it is the part of this regulation a member of the public is most
   likely to need.
4. **Go through one registered intermediary, exclusively**, and leave the
   offering publicly visible on its platform for at least
   [21 days](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L231) before any sale.
5. **File a Form C** with the prescribed disclosure, including financial
   statements at a level of assurance set by how much is being raised.

Then there are three duties that continue after the money is in:

6. **Say almost nothing.** Rule 204 permits one narrow notice pointing at the
   platform, and nothing more.
7. **File an annual report** — Form C-AR, within
   [120 days](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L235) of each fiscal year
   end — until one of five exit conditions occurs.
8. **Buyers may not resell** for one year, subject to four exceptions.

The distinction between the first five and the last three is not stylistic, and
it is easy to miss on a first reading. Rule 100(a)(4) carries a proviso: failure
to comply with the ongoing reporting rules **does not cost the issuer the
exemption for the offering already made**. A reporting default is a violation
without being a retroactive disqualifier. It reappears instead as a Rule
100(b)(5) disqualifier for the issuer's _next_ offering.
