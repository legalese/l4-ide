Having raised the money, the issuer owes an annual report — Form C-AR — filed
within [120 days](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L235) of each fiscal
year end, and it keeps owing one every year until one of five things happens:

1. it becomes a reporting company under the Exchange Act;
2. it has filed at least one annual report and has fewer than
   [300](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L220) holders of record;
3. it has filed at least three annual reports and has total assets not exceeding
   [$10,000,000](src:jl4/examples/legal/regcf/regcf.l4#L225);
4. it repurchases all the securities sold under the exemption;
5. it liquidates or dissolves.

When one of those occurs the annual duty converts into a single closing duty: a
Form C-TR terminating the reporting obligation, within five business days.

A published summary the corpus was checked against gives three of those five
exit conditions.
