# Notes on judgement calls (vanilla, t2)

- **Q1 / Q8 (firefighter duty / military training exercise).** Read "duty as a firefighter" and
  "military training exercise" as falling squarely within the §2.1 exclusions for "Service as a
  fire fighter" and "Service in the military" — a training exercise is part of military service,
  and the injury is explicitly tied to performing the duty, not merely coincidental with the
  person's occupation. Both -> excluded.

- **Q4 (confirmation given at 8 months).** §1.3 requires written confirmation of the wellness
  visit no later than the 7th month anniversary. Confirmation supplied at 8 months breaches that
  deadline outright — read this as failing "in a timely fashion" under §1.1(3)/§1.2, regardless of
  when the underlying visit itself occurred, since the supply action itself is late on its face.
  This triggers deemed cancelation under §1.2, so the policy is not in effect.

- **Q5 (punching own face to show off, no fraud).** This was the closest call. The contract's
  §2.1 exclusion list does not mention self-inflicted or intentionally-caused injury at all, and
  the query explicitly negates the one condition that *is* textually tied to conduct-based
  cancelation (fraud/misrepresentation, §1.2). Rather than infer an unstated "self-harm" exclusion
  that real-world policies often carry but this one does not textually contain, I treated the
  enumerated exclusions in §2.1 as exhaustive per the standing preamble ("no other exclusions
  apply … than … referenced in the query") and answered Yes. I considered "I do not know" here
  (turning on whether "accidental injury" in §1.1 implicitly excludes intentionally-caused harm),
  but the absence of any textual hook for a self-harm exclusion, plus the query's specific framing
  around fraud, made Yes the better-supported reading from the text as given.

- **Q6 (skydiving, age 79, wellness proof at 6.5 months).** Skydiving is independently excluded
  under §2.1(1), so the outcome (No) does not depend on resolving whether "6.5 months" refers to
  the confirmation-submission date (7-month deadline, satisfied) or could imply the underlying
  visit occurred after the separate 6-month "occurring no later than" mark. Flagged but immaterial.

- **Q9 (son biting ankle, serving as a police officer at the time).** Read the §2.1(4) "Service in
  the police" exclusion as requiring a causal link ("arising directly or indirectly out of" that
  service) between the service and the injury, not merely that the claimant happens to hold that
  occupation at the time of hospitalization. A domestic bite injury from one's son has no stated
  connection to police duties, so the exclusion does not apply. Wellness proof at 6 months is
  within both the 6-month and 7-month marks in §1.3, so no timeliness issue either.

- **§1.1(3) "still pending."** For scenarios where hospitalization occurs before the 7-month
  confirmation deadline has elapsed (e.g. Q3 at 5 months) and no confirmation timing is otherwise
  given, read the §1.3 condition as "still pending" (not yet due), which under §1.1(3) keeps the
  policy in effect on its own — no assumption that confirmation was already supplied was needed.
