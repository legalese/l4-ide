# Notes — vanilla t1

Judgement calls made while answering directly from the contract text (Sections 1-3 only;
the furnished text stops at 3.6 and never reaches a Section 4 benefits schedule or Section 5
policy schedule — neither was needed for any of the nine queries, since all nine turn on
"is the policy in effect" (§1) and "does an exclusion apply" (§2), both fully present).

1. **Q1 (firefighter burns).** "while doing my duty as a firefighter" is a direct causal match
   to exclusion 2.1.3 ("service as a fire fighter"). No.

2. **Q3 vs Q4 (wellness-visit timing — the two branches of §1.1(3)/§1.3/§1.2).**
   - Q3: hospitalization at month 5, before the 7-month confirmation deadline has even arrived.
     Read this as the condition being "still pending" under §1.1(3), which keeps the policy in
     effect. Yes.
   - Q4: confirmation actually given at month 8, i.e. after the 7-month deadline in §1.3 had
     already lapsed. Read this as "not satisfied in a timely fashion," which §1.2 deems a
     cancellation — regardless of when the underlying wellness visit itself took place, since
     the late act is the *submission*, not the visit. No. ("Traveling abroad" is not a live
     issue given §3.1.1's worldwide, 24/7 coverage — treated as a non-trick detail.)

3. **Q5 (punching own face to show off).** Not on the closed exclusion list in §2.1. Treated
   "accidental injury" under the usual insurance-law reading that looks at whether the *result*
   (getting hurt) was intended, not just whether the underlying act (throwing the punch) was
   voluntary — so a foolish, unintended injury from showing off still counts as accidental.
   Also weighted the query's explicit "I did not commit fraud or misrepresentation" as closing
   off the one plausible alternative bar to coverage (§1.2's fraud/misrepresentation
   cancellation ground); if that clarification weren't meant to be dispositive, it would be an
   odd detail to include. Yes.

4. **Q8 vs Q9 (occupational status vs. causation under "arising directly or indirectly out
   of").**
   - Q8: injury sustained *in a military training exercise* — the training exercise is itself a
     military-service activity, i.e. a direct causal link to exclusion 2.1.2. The reassurances
     given ("within the policy term," "did not commit fraud") close off unrelated cancellation
     theories but don't touch the military exclusion. No.
   - Q9: injury caused by "my son biting me in the ankle" — a domestic/personal event with no
     causal connection to police duties, even though the claimant happens to hold police-officer
     status and was on duty at the time. Read "arising directly or indirectly out of ... service
     in the police" as requiring some causal nexus to policing activity, not mere on-duty
     coincidence. Yes.

5. **Q6 (skydiving + age 79 + timely wellness proof).** Treated the single matching exclusion
   (skydiving, 2.1.1) as sufficient to deny the claim on its own — the other two facts (age 79
   is under the 80 threshold; wellness proof at 6.5 months is inside the 7-month window) are
   satisfied but don't rescue a claim once any one exclusion applies. No.

6. **Q2, Q7.** Read straightforwardly off §2.1(5) (age 78 < 80) and the absence of any matching
   exclusion for a heart attack with timely (month-2) wellness proof, respectively, per the
   standing preamble that no unreferenced exclusion applies. Both Yes.

No query was answered "I do not know" — every fact pattern maps onto text actually present in
the furnished sections.
