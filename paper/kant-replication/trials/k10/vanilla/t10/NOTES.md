# Notes on judgement calls (t10, vanilla cell)

These are the points where the contract text required an interpretive choice rather than a
mechanical lookup.

## 1.3 has two separate deadlines, not one

Section 1.3 reads: "No later than the 7th month anniversary ... you will supply us with written
confirmation ... of a wellness visit ... occurring no later than the 6th month anniversary."

I read this as two distinct temporal facts bundled into one sentence:
- the wellness **visit** itself must occur by month 6;
- **written confirmation** of it must be supplied by month 7.

Section 1.1(3) keeps the policy in effect if 1.3 "is still pending or has been satisfied in a
timely fashion." Several queries state only "proof was provided at month X," without separately
stating when the underlying visit occurred. Where X is unambiguous relative to both deadlines
(e.g. "2 months," "6 months" — proof necessarily follows the visit, so proof-by-month-6 forces
visit-by-month-6 too) I treated 1.3 as satisfied. Where X was between 6 and 7 (e.g. "6.5 months"),
there is a genuine ambiguity about whether the underlying visit itself was on time — but this only
came up in Q6, where the skydiving exclusion is independently dispositive, so it didn't affect the
answer. Where X exceeds 7 (Q4: "8 months"), the confirmation deadline is breached outright
regardless of visit timing, which I treated as unambiguous.

Q4 also assumes that "confirmation ... given 8 months after the effective date" describes the
operative timeline for the claim being evaluated (i.e., the condition had already failed by the
time coverage is being assessed), since the query gives no independent, earlier date for the
hospitalization itself.

## §2.1 exclusions 1-4 require a causal link; item 5 (age) does not

The chapeau of §2.1 excludes an event "arising directly or indirectly out of" the five listed
items. Items 1-4 (skydiving, military, firefighting, police) are activities, and the "arising ...
out of" language reads as requiring some causal nexus between the activity and the
sickness/injury. Item 5 (age ≥ 80) is a bare status condition, not something an injury "arises out
of," so I read it as an unconditional bar independent of cause.

This distinction drove Q9: the query states the claimant "was serving as a police officer at the
time of hospitalization," but the stated cause of the injury (being bitten by his own son) has no
causal connection to police duties. I read this as deliberately parallel-but-distinct from Q1 and
Q8, where the cause is explicitly tied to the excluded activity ("burns suffered while doing my
duty as a firefighter"; "injured in a military training exercise") — the phrase "at the time of"
in Q9 signals mere concurrent status, not causation, so the police exclusion does not reach it.
This is a judgement call; the contract does not spell out a causation test in so many words.

## Q5 ("punching my own face to show off") — answered "I do not know"

Coverage under §1.1 is limited to hospitalization "for sickness or accidental injury." Nothing in
the text supplied defines "accidental injury," and no definitions section is present in this
excerpt. Whether a hospitalization resulting from a deliberate, voluntary act (punching oneself),
where the injury's severity was presumably unintended, counts as "accidental" is genuinely
contestable on ordinary usage grounds — and it isn't resolved by the closed list of specific
exclusions in §2.1, which does not mention self-inflicted or intentional acts at all. The query's
explicit disclaimer of fraud/misrepresentation only forecloses the §1.2 cancellation route; it
does not resolve the antecedent question of whether this event falls within the coverage grant in
the first place. I treated this as a case where the contract as given genuinely does not determine
the answer, rather than guess.

## Other facts treated as non-issues

- Q4's "traveling abroad": §3.1.1 states the policy covers the insured "twenty-four (24) hours a
  day anywhere in the world," so foreign travel is not itself a problem — the query's real issue
  is the late confirmation, not the location.
- Q8's "hospitalization occurred within the policy term" and "I did not commit fraud," and Q9's
  timely wellness-visit proof: read as ruling out alternative denial theories (policy lapse, fraud)
  so that the sole live issue in each is the one exclusion actually analyzed above.
- Noticed but not decision-relevant: §1.2 refers to "the policy term described in Section 5 below"
  for the automatic-cancellation-at-term-end trigger, but the policy term is actually stated in
  §3.6 in this excerpt. This numbering mismatch didn't affect any of the nine answers.
