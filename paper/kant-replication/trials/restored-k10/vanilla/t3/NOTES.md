# Notes on judgement calls (vanilla trial, no encoding)

These are the places where the contract text required an interpretive choice rather than a
mechanical lookup.

## Q1, Q6, Q8 — direct exclusion hits

Firefighter duty (Q1), skydiving (Q6), and military training (Q8) each fall squarely inside the
"arising directly or indirectly out of" language of §3.1's five enumerated exclusions. Treated
these as unambiguous. Extra facts in the queries (age 79 in Q6, "within the policy term" and "no
fraud" in Q8) are red herrings that don't change the outcome once the primary exclusion fires.

## Q3 — condition still "pending" is a valid state, not a defect

§1.1(3) says the policy is in effect if the §1.3 wellness-confirmation condition "is still pending
or has been satisfied in a timely fashion." At 5 months post-effective-date the 7-month deadline
hasn't arrived yet, so "still pending" applies and does not, by itself, threaten coverage. I read
this as deliberately built into the contract to distinguish "not yet due" from "overdue."

## Q4 — two independent grounds, both pointing the same way

Two separate provisions cut against coverage here, and I didn't have to choose between them:

- Confirmation given at 8 months is later than the §1.3/§1.2 deadline (7th month anniversary),
  which §1.2 treats as an automatic cancellation ("not satisfied in a timely fashion").
- Separately, §2.2 limits the payable benefit to confinement "in a hospital in the United States."
  I read "hospitalized due to a fall while traveling abroad" as the hospitalization itself
  happening abroad, not merely the injury. §4.1.1 ("insures You ... anywhere in the world") speaks
  to where a covered event can _occur_, not to where the _hospital_ must be for the daily benefit
  to be payable — I treated these as two different clauses answering two different questions,
  not as contradicting each other.
  Judgement call: I assumed the query's "I had given confirmation ... 8 months after" temporally
  precedes the hospitalization it's describing (i.e., by the time hospitalization is at issue, the
  late-confirmation fact has already occurred), rather than reading them as unordered/simultaneous.

## Q5 — the load-bearing word is "accidental," not any exclusion

This was the hardest call. There is no exclusion in §3.1 for self-inflicted or intentional injury,
so a pure exclusion-list reading could suggest "Yes." But coverage in §2.1 is granted only for
"sickness or accidental Injury" — "accidental" is a threshold element of the coverage grant itself,
not merely subject to the exclusion list. Deliberately punching your own face to show off is a
voluntary, intended act that directly produces the harm (unlike, say, a skydiving mishap, where the
activity is voluntary but the resulting injury is still an unintended mishap — which is presumably
why the drafters had to exclude skydiving/military/police/firefighting by name rather than relying
on "accidental" to do that work). I read "and I did not commit fraud or misrepresentation" in the
query as a deliberate red herring pointing toward §1.2's cancellation ground, distracting from the
real issue, which is that the injury never clears the "accidental" threshold in §2.1 at all.
Landed on **No**, but flag this as the least certain of the nine.

## Q9 — occupation is not the same as causation

§3.1(4) excludes injury "arising directly or indirectly out of ... Service in the police." Being a
police officer at the time of the incident is occupational status, not causation; a son biting an
ankle has no stated connection to police duties. Read the exclusion as requiring a causal nexus to
the service, not merely contemporaneous employment, by parallel with how §3.1(3) is written for
firefighting in Q1 (there, the query explicitly supplies the nexus — "while doing my duty as a
firefighter" — which Q9 conspicuously does not for policing). Wellness proof at 6 months is inside
the 7-month deadline, so §1.3 is satisfied. Answer: Yes.

## General assumption applied throughout

Where a query states only when wellness-visit _proof was provided_ (Q6: 6.5 mo, Q7: 2 mo, Q9: 6 mo)
and says nothing about when the underlying visit itself occurred, I assumed the visit occurred on
time (by the 6-month mark) as an "other condition… satisfied" per the standing instructions, since
none of the queries put that specific fact in dispute. This assumption was only outcome-determinative
nowhere by itself (Q6 is independently excluded via skydiving), but it is baked into the Q7 and Q9
"Yes" answers.
