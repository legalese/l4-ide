# Notes — judgement calls (vanilla, t6)

Answered directly from `inputs/chubb-policy.txt` and `inputs/queries-blind.md`, applying the
standing preamble ("assume all other conditions are met and no other exclusions apply, where
'other' means anything not referenced in the query") plus the task's own assumptions (signed,
premium paid on time, all unreferenced conditions satisfied).

Points that required judgement rather than a direct textual lookup:

- **Q4 (wellness-visit confirmation given 8 months after the effective date).** §1.3 requires
  confirmation "no later than the 7th month anniversary." 8 months is after that deadline, so the
  condition was not "satisfied in a timely fashion," which under §1.2 means "cancelation will be
  deemed to have occurred." I treated this as dispositive (No), independent of the secondary
  wrinkle that the fall happened "while traveling abroad": §2.2 limits the Daily Hospital Income
  Benefit to confinement "in a hospital in the United States," which sits in tension with §4.1's
  "insures You ... anywhere in the world." I did not need to resolve that tension because the
  timing violation alone is enough, but flagging it since a different reading of "abroad" could
  independently matter.

- **Q5 (punching own face to show off for friends; no fraud/misrepresentation).** The contract
  never defines "accidental" and §3.1's General Exclusions is a closed list of five specific
  causes (skydiving, military, firefighting, police, age ≥ 80) — self-inflicted or intentional
  conduct is not among them. The query's explicit "I did not commit fraud or misrepresentation"
  reads as ruling out the only other textual hook (§1.2's fraud/misrepresentation cancellation
  ground). Reading §3.1 as exhaustive and construing the undefined "accidental Injury" in the
  insured's favor, I answered Yes. A stricter reading of "accidental" as requiring the triggering
  act itself (not just the outcome) to be unintended would point the other way; the contract text
  does not settle which reading controls, so this is the least certain answer in the set.

- **Q9 (son biting my ankle; serving as a police officer at the time).** §3.1.4 excludes events
  "arising directly or indirectly out of ... service in the police." I read this as requiring a
  causal link between the injury and police service, not mere occupational status at the moment of
  injury. A domestic bite from one's own son has no stated connection to police duties, so I did
  not apply the exclusion. Combined with a timely wellness-visit confirmation (6 months, inside the
  7-month deadline), I answered Yes.

- **Q3, Q6, Q7, Q9 — "wellness visit" timing.** §1.3's deadline is 7 months for supplying written
  confirmation (of a visit occurring by 6 months). Where a query gives a submission time under 7
  months (Q3: visit condition not yet due at 5 months; Q6: 6.5 months; Q7: 2 months; Q9: 6 months),
  I treated §1.3 as "still pending" or "satisfied in a timely fashion" per §1.1(3), i.e. not a
  cancellation trigger. Only Q4's 8-month figure exceeds the deadline.

- **Q1, Q6, Q8 — enumerated-exclusion cases.** Firefighting duty (Q1), skydiving (Q6), and a
  military training exercise (Q8) map directly onto §3.1 items 3, 1, and 2 respectively; these
  were treated as clear-cut No answers regardless of the other facts recited in each query (age,
  wellness-visit timing, absence of fraud), since §3.1 excludes the event by cause independent of
  whether the policy is otherwise in force.
