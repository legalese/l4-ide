# Notes — judgement calls (vanilla, t2)

These are the points where the contract text required an interpretive choice rather than a
mechanical lookup. Everything else followed directly from §1 (policy in effect), §2 (benefits),
and §3.1's five-item exclusion list.

## Q3 — pneumonia, 5 months in, age 65
No fact about the §1.3 wellness-visit / confirmation requirement is given in the query. Per the
task's standing instruction ("assume all other conditions are satisfied ... unless explicitly
referenced"), I treated §1.3 as satisfied or still pending. At the 5-month mark this is true either
way, since the 6- and 7-month anniversaries haven't passed yet, so §1.1(3)'s "still pending" branch
covers it regardless.

## Q4 — fall abroad, wellness confirmation given at 8 months
Two candidate issues here; I treated the second as dispositive:
- §4.1.1 says the policy insures the person "twenty-four (24) hours a day anywhere in the world,"
  so the fact that the fall happened abroad does not by itself disqualify the event. (§2.2's
  separate restriction of the *benefit payment* to confinement "in a hospital in the United
  States" might matter if the person was hospitalized abroad, but the query doesn't say where the
  confinement itself occurred, so I didn't treat that as an independent ground for denial.)
- §1.3 requires written confirmation of the wellness visit no later than the 7th month
  anniversary. Confirmation given at 8 months is after that deadline, so it was not "satisfied in
  a timely fashion," which under §1.2 means cancelation "will be deemed to have occurred." That
  reads as dispositive on its own: the policy was not in effect at the relevant time, so the claim
  is not covered.

## Q5 — punching own face to show off, no fraud/misrepresentation
This is the one I could not resolve from the text alone, so I answered "I do not know" rather than
guess.
- The query's explicit "I did not commit fraud or misrepresentation" rules out cancelation under
  §1.2's fraud/misrepresentation prong.
- What's left is whether this is an "accidental Injury" under §2.1. The contract never defines
  "accidental," and §3.1's exclusion list is a closed, specific set (skydiving, military service,
  firefighting, police service, age ≥ 80) with no catch-all for self-inflicted or intentional
  injury. That omission could cut either way: read one way, the exclusion list is exhaustive and
  silence means it's covered; read the other way, "accidental" itself is doing the work, and a
  deliberate act (punching one's own face), even one undertaken without intent to cause the
  resulting hospitalization, may not qualify as an "accident" in the first place. The contract
  gives no definition to settle which reading governs, so I treated this as a genuine gap rather
  than picking a side.

## Q6 — skydiving, age 79, wellness proof at 6.5 months
Excluded outright by §3.1.1 (skydiving), so the wellness-visit timing and the age (79 < 80) don't
change the outcome. I did not need to resolve whether "proof provided at 6.5 months" also implies
the underlying visit itself happened by the 6-month anniversary (see Q9 below), since the skydiving
exclusion is dispositive either way.

## Q7 — heart attack, wellness proof at 2 months, age 75
Straightforward: sickness under §2.1, not on the §3.1 exclusion list, age under 80, wellness
confirmation well inside both the 6- and 7-month deadlines.

## Q8 — military training exercise, no fraud
Read "injured in a military training exercise" as squarely within §3.1.2 ("Service in the
military"). The query's "hospitalization occurred within the policy term" and "I did not commit
fraud" address §1.1/§1.2 (is the policy in effect, was it canceled) but don't touch the §3.1
exclusion, which applies independently of whether the policy is otherwise in effect.

## Q9 — son biting ankle, wellness proof at 6 months, serving as police officer
Two judgement calls:
- §1.3: "proof of my wellness visit was provided 6 months after the effective date" — I read this
  as satisfying §1.3, on the inference that proof of a visit cannot precede the visit itself, so a
  visit whose proof was already delivered by month 6 must itself have occurred at or before month
  6, which also clears the visit's own 6-month deadline.
- §3.1.4 ("Service in the police"): the exclusion applies to an event causing injury "arising
  directly or indirectly out of" police service. Being bitten in the ankle by one's own son is a
  domestic incident with no apparent causal connection, direct or indirect, to police duties. I
  read "I was serving as a police officer at the time of hospitalization" as background occupational
  status, not as a claim that the injury arose from that service, and distinguished this from Q1
  and Q8, which each state the injury arose *while performing* the duty in question (firefighting
  duty, a military training exercise). On that reading the exclusion does not reach Q9.
