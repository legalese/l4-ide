# Notes — vanilla / t4

Judgement calls made while answering directly from the contract text (no encoding), per TASK.md.

## Q1 (firefighter burns) and Q8 (military training injury)

Read "burns suffered while doing my duty as a firefighter" and "injured in a military training
exercise" as satisfying the causal test in §3.1 ("any event ... arising directly or indirectly out
of ... Service in the military ... Service as a fire fighter"). Both exclusions are triggered
because the stated cause of the injury is the excluded service itself. -> No.

## Q4 (fall abroad + wellness confirmation at 8 months)

Two independent grounds, either of which is sufficient on its own:

1. §1.3 requires written confirmation of the wellness visit "no later than the 7th month
   anniversary." Confirmation given at 8 months is late, so the condition was not "satisfied in a
   timely fashion" -> §1.2 deems the policy canceled, and §1.1 makes payment conditional on the
   policy being in effect at the time of hospitalization.
2. §2.2 says the Daily Hospital Income Benefit "will only be payable for each (24 hour) day of
   continuous confinement in a hospital in the United States." Being hospitalized abroad does not
   meet this. This sits in tension with §4.1.1 ("Your Policy insures You ... anywhere in the
   world"), which I read as governing where a covered _event_ can occur, not as overriding §2.2's
   specific US-hospital condition on _payability_ of this particular benefit. Judgement call: I
   treated the more specific clause (§2.2) as controlling over the general one (§4.1.1) for
   whether the benefit is payable, rather than treating this as an unresolvable conflict.
   Both point to No, so I did not need to rely on either alone.

## Q5 (punching own face to show off, no fraud/misrepresentation)

The contract's General Exclusions (§3.1) do not list self-inflicted or intentional injury, and the
question explicitly stipulates no fraud/misrepresentation (closing off the §1.2 cancelation route).
The remaining question is whether a deliberate act (punching oneself, "to show off") produces an
"accidental Injury" within the meaning of §2.1 at all. This is the judgement call I'm least certain
of: the contract does not define "accidental," and there is a real textual argument for "Yes"
(expressio unius — the drafters itemized specific exclusions and did not include self-harm, so
under a narrow reading nothing in the text bars it). I resolved it as "No" on the ordinary/common
meaning that an injury caused by one's own deliberate act is not "accidental" — the voluntariness of
the punch defeats the base coverage grant rather than triggering an exclusion. I considered "I do
not know" for this one specifically but judged the fact pattern (intentional act, stated motive)
strong enough to point one way.

## Q9 (son bit ankle; serving as police officer at time of hospitalization)

Contrasted this against Q1/Q8. §3.1 excludes injury "arising directly or indirectly out of ...
Service in the police" — a causal test. The question states occupational status "at the time of
hospitalization," not that the injury arose from police duty (unlike Q1's "while doing my duty as a
firefighter" or Q8's "injured in a military training exercise," which are explicitly causal). A
son biting his parent's ankle has no stated connection to police work, so I read the exclusion as
not triggered. Wellness proof "provided 6 months after the effective date" satisfies both the
visit deadline (no later than the 6th month anniversary) and the confirmation deadline (no later
than the 7th month anniversary). -> Yes.

## §1.3's two deadlines (visit vs. confirmation)

§1.3 sets two dates: the wellness visit itself must occur no later than the 6-month anniversary,
and written confirmation of it must be supplied no later than the 7-month anniversary. Where a
query only states when confirmation/proof was "provided" or "submitted," I treated the visit as
necessarily having occurred on or before that same date (you cannot get confirmation of a visit
that hasn't happened yet), which was sufficient to resolve every query that turned on this
(Q3 — still pending at 5 months; Q7 — proof at 2 months; Q9 — proof at 6 months; Q4 — proof at 8
months, already late regardless of when the underlying visit occurred). Q6's wellness timing
(6.5 months) did not end up affecting its answer since §3.1's skydiving exclusion is dispositive on
its own.

## Q2, Q3, Q7 — baseline positive cases

Age thresholds (78, 65, 75) are all below the "equal to or greater than 80" cutoff in §3.1.5, no
excluded activity is mentioned, and (where relevant) the §1.3 wellness-visit timeline is either not
yet due or was met on time. No exclusion applies -> Yes in each case.
