\section{Instantiation in L4}
\label{sec:tooling}

It helps to begin with what the tool deliberately does \emph{not} represent. A
decision-theoretic ``ought'' in the manner of \src{Pearl 1993} composes four ingredients: a
causal structure of acts and their effects, the conditions under which a choice is posed, a
\emph{probability} over outcomes, and a \emph{utility} on them. L4 makes two of these
explicit and computable---the act-and-effect structure, as the \texttt{HENCE}/\texttt{LEST}
graph, and the conditions, as the constitutive \texttt{IF}/\texttt{AND}/\texttt{OR} decision
logic (its ``terms and conditions'')---and drops the third outright: there is no probability
anywhere in the language. Utility re-enters only at the assertion level, and only ordinally,
as the party ordering $\ppref$ of Section~\ref{sec:boundary}. L4 is in this sense the
\emph{no-probability limit} of qualitative decision theory, and it is \emph{entitled} to
that limit because legal consequences are largely \emph{constituted, not caused} (the
distinction of Section~\ref{sec:sorter}): ``the deadline lapses, hence breach'' is a
stipulation of the rule, certain by definition, not a bet on the world. Where the law does
turn on an uncertain fact, the uncertainty is pushed down to fact-finding---\emph{did the
antecedent obtain?}---not up into the consequence. This is also why the all-paths dominator
of Section~\ref{sec:nec} is so much as available: one can model-check a reachability graph,
but not a probability distribution, into a yes-or-no landmark.

The two-level architecture of this paper is not only a way of talking; its lower level is
implemented. We report here, with deliberate care to separate what \emph{ships} from what
is \emph{designed}, how the construction lands in L4, the legal DSL whose evaluator and
tooling are open source.\footnote{The constructs described here live in the \texttt{jl4}
implementation; the regulative parser, the lazy evaluator, and the state-graph extractor
are in the \texttt{jl4-core} package, and the decision service that exposes them over HTTP
is in \texttt{jl4-service}. We name modules rather than line numbers, which drift.}

\paragraph{The letter ships.} The object level of Section~\ref{sec:object}---a contract as
a labelled transition system with neutral terminals---is realised directly. L4's regulative
form names a party, a deontic modal, an action, an optional guard and deadline, and the two
continuations:
\begin{verbatim}
    PARTY  p
    MUST   a                 -- or MAY, SHANT, or the neutral DO
    WITHIN deadline
    PROVIDED guard
    HENCE  outcome_if_done
    LEST   outcome_if_not
\end{verbatim}
The terminals are exactly Meyer's neutral markers: \texttt{FULFILLED}, and
\texttt{BREACH BY}~$p$~\texttt{BECAUSE}~$r$, a breach annotated with a structured reason but
carrying no verdict of its own. The evaluator compiles a regulative program to an
\emph{obligation value} and, given a dated sequence of events, runs it: a directive
\texttt{\#TRACE}~$c$~\texttt{AT}~$t$~\texttt{WITH}~$e_1,\dots,e_n$ simulates the contract
$c$ against those events and reduces it either to \texttt{FULFILLED} or to a structured
breach---a missed deadline or an explicit \texttt{BREACH}---with a full, replayable trace.
The companion directives \texttt{\#EVAL}, \texttt{\#CHECK} (type-check only), and
\texttt{\#ASSERT} round out an audit-grade evaluation surface. This is the executable
``letter,'' and it is the artefact a non-lawyer actually runs.

\paragraph{The state graph ships---and it is the substrate the spirit needs.} More than
evaluate a single trace, the toolchain \emph{extracts} from a regulative program the
labelled transition system itself: a graph whose states are typed (an initial state,
intermediate states, and the two terminal kinds, fulfilled and breach) and whose edges are
labelled by modal, party, action, deadline, and guard, with \texttt{HENCE} and \texttt{LEST}
distinguished as the success and failure branches. The extractor renders this to GraphViz
and serves it over the decision-service API. The significance for this paper is precise:
the object $T=(S,A,{\to},s_0)$ of Definition~\ref{def:dom} is not a modelling convenience we
impose from outside but a structure the tool already builds and hands back. The ``letter as
a machine of states and admissible actions'' is a concrete, inspectable artefact.

\paragraph{What does not yet ship, and why saying so is part of the contribution.} The
bounded \MUST{} of Section~\ref{sec:nec} is a \emph{query} over that extracted graph---the
dominator set $\dom_s(J)$---and that query is, at the time of writing, \emph{not}
implemented. We are candid about this because the boundary is the paper's subject: what we
add over the shipping tool is exactly the derived-necessity reading, and realising it is a
near-linear dominator computation \src{Lengauer \& Tarjan 1979} over a graph that already
exists in memory, not new infrastructure. Two further layers are designed but unbuilt. The
\emph{verification} of the faithful region (Section~\ref{sec:faithful})---closed goal
family, acyclic ordering, closed facts---is to be discharged by transpiling the extracted
automaton to an external model checker (UPPAAL, SPIN, or NuSMV) and posing the bounded
modality as a temporal-logic property; this is specified in L4's roadmap but not yet
wired. And the party-indexed ordering $\ppref$ of Section~\ref{sec:boundary}, together with
the force-gap, are today \emph{parameters supplied to} the reading rather than quantities the
tool computes. The honest summary is a single sentence: \emph{the object level and its state
graph are implemented and in use; the dominator query and the verification backends are
designed and not yet built.} The gap between those two clauses is, almost exactly, the gap
this paper exists to map.

\paragraph{What it already buys.} Even short of the dominator query, the shipping layer is
not idle with respect to the thesis. Because the letter executes and the state graph is
inspectable, an obligation can be \emph{simulated} and its breach traced rather than merely
asserted; because the evaluation is total and replayable, it can serve as the
guard-railed, audit-grade ``left brain'' behind a neural-network front end---an LLM that
proposes, and an L4 trace that certifies. The derived \MUST{} is the next increment on this
substrate, not a separate system.

\section{A worked example: perfection under UCC Article~9}
\label{sec:eval}

We close the loop on a single doctrine in which the bounded \MUST{} is not a contrivance
but the law's own structure: perfection of a security interest under Article~9 of the
Uniform Commercial Code. The example is chosen because it exhibits, in real and citable
law, the precise phenomenon of Section~\ref{sec:nec}---a goal reachable by several methods
in general, collapsing to a \emph{unique necessary} method under particular
circumstances---and because the collapse is \emph{non-temporal}, driven by the kind of
collateral rather than by a deadline.

\paragraph{The doctrine.} A secured party perfects its interest to obtain priority over
competing claimants; perfection is the goal $J$. Article~9 offers, in general, three
methods: \emph{filing} a financing statement (UCC \S\,9-310, the default),
\emph{possession} of the collateral (\S\,9-313), and \emph{control} (\S\,9-314). For
ordinary goods, more than one method is available, so none is necessary. But for a
\emph{deposit account} taken as original collateral, \S\,9-312(b)(1) provides that the
interest ``may be perfected only by control,'' and possession is not available at all. The
general menu of methods collapses to one.

\paragraph{The object level in L4.} We encode only the letter: the methods as a neutral
choice point and the outcome each produces, with no \MUST{} written by hand (declarations
elided).
\begin{verbatim}
    PARTY secured_party DO file
      HENCE perfected
      LEST  PARTY secured_party DO possess
              HENCE perfected
              LEST  PARTY secured_party DO control
                      HENCE perfected
                      LEST  unperfected
\end{verbatim}
Which edges actually reach \texttt{perfected} is a fact about the collateral, supplied by
the statute, not a deontic operator we annotate. The extractor of
Section~\ref{sec:tooling} yields two automata for the two collateral types:
\begin{verbatim}
    ordinary goods                     deposit account (9-312(b)(1))
       file ----> perfected               file ----> unperfected
    s0 possess -> perfected            s0 possess -> unperfected
       control -> perfected               control -> perfected
\end{verbatim}
The bounded modalities of Definition~\ref{def:dom} are now read off, \emph{not declared},
by the dominator query over each graph:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Ordinary goods.} Three disjoint edges reach \texttt{perfected}; no action
  lies on every path, so $\dom_{s_0}(\texttt{perfected})=\varnothing$. Each method is a
  \MAY{} ($\EF\,\texttt{perfected}$ holds via any of them); none is a \MUST{}.
  \item \textbf{Deposit account.} Filing and possession reach only \texttt{unperfected};
  the unique path to \texttt{perfected} runs through \texttt{control}, so
  $\dom_{s_0}(\texttt{perfected})=\{\texttt{control}\}$. Control is a \MUST{}---derived
  entirely from the pruned graph and the goal, with no deontic primitive in the source.
\end{itemize}
This is the $\MAY\!\to\!\MUST$ transition of Section~\ref{sec:nec} in its non-temporal
form: nothing expired; the set of alternatives was narrowed by the \emph{kind} of
collateral, and control entered $\dom_{s_0}(\texttt{perfected})$ as the last alternative
fell away. The constitutive/regulative sorter of Section~\ref{sec:sorter} also earns its
keep here: ``may be perfected only by control'' is a \emph{must-be}, a condition on the
institutional fact of perfection, not a duty the secured party breaches---and the account
classifies it correctly, as bare domination with no valued breach terminal.

\paragraph{The boundary, on the same example.} The two orderings of
Section~\ref{sec:holmeshart} and the force-gap of Section~\ref{sec:force} are visible too.
The law's ordering $\plaw$ ranks \texttt{perfected} categorically above
\texttt{unperfected}. A given secured party's $\ppref$ may track it or diverge: a lender
who needs to prevail against a lien creditor or the debtor's bankruptcy trustee (UCC
\S\,9-317; the trustee's strong-arm power) faces a large gap between perfecting and not, and
for it the ``must control'' carries real force; a party content with a merely attached,
enforceable-against-the-debtor interest assigns the two outcomes nearly equal $\ppref$-rank,
and for it the same ``must'' is, in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:force}, close to a price.
One letter; force indexed to party; the divergence between $\plaw$ and $\ppref$ doing the
Holmes/Hart work---on a fragment that L4 parses, type-checks, evaluates, and draws today,
needing only the dominator query of Section~\ref{sec:tooling} to read the \MUST{} off
automatically.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have treated ``or else what?'' not as a cynic's challenge to obligation but as its
analysis. The deontic force of a rule is bounded---by a goal, by a consequence, and by the
ordering against which compliance and breach are ranked---and the binding can be made
precise on a transition-system ``letter'' over which the ``spirit'' is read as a query. We
claimed no new logical primitive, and conceded each ingredient by name: the
Anderson--Meyer reduction of obligation to a modality plus a neutral violation, planning
landmarks and their dominator computation, the Kratzer ordering source, betterness-and-
priority orderings, and obligation as strategic ability. The contribution is their
composition and its jurisprudential reading: a party-indexed ordering laid over a
\emph{graph-dominator} necessity; the sanction/goal duality as the \emph{sign} of an
outcome under that ordering; the force of a ``must'' as an ordinal preference gap, which
predicts both ``a fine is a price'' and the protester's inversion; and, above all, the
unification of Holmes's bad man and Hart's internal point of view as the divergence and the
coincidence of a \emph{single} pair of orderings over one derived necessity. UCC
Article~9 showed the bounded \MUST{} arising as the law's own structure rather than our
imposition, and the L4 toolchain showed the letter and its state graph already in hand,
with the derived-necessity query the next increment over a shipping substrate.

Three lines of work remain. The basket question---necessity for a conjunction of
goals---needs minimal-hitting-set enumeration \src{Reiter 1987} rather than the Galois
lattice that Remark~\ref{rem:basket} refutes. The multi-party escalation of
Section~\ref{sec:atl}, from cooperative domination to strategic ability, is a recombination
we have flagged but not made. And the verification backend that would discharge the
faithful region of Section~\ref{sec:faithful} at compile time is specified but unbuilt.
Each is a facet of the same programme: to give law the tooling that lets a ``must'' be
asked, and answered, \emph{or else what?}
