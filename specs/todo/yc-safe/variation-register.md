## C. Side letters

The US **Pro Rata Agreement** (`Pro Rata Side Letter.md`, 29 lines) in five points:

- **What it grants.** A right, defined as the `Pro Rata Right`, `to purchase its pro rata share of Standard Preferred Stock being sold in the Equity Financing`. It is a purchase right in the priced round, not a conversion term, which is why it is a separate instrument.
- **The formula.** `pro rata share = (Capital Stock issued on conversion of ALL of the Investor's Safes bearing a “Post-Money Valuation Cap”) / (Company Capitalization)`. Both inputs are defined in the Safe, not here: `Capitalized terms used herein shall have the meanings set forth in the Investor’s Safe.`
- **Who qualifies.** Only a holder of a Safe that has a `“Post-Money Valuation Cap”`. Discount-only and MFN-only Safes contribute nothing to the numerator, so a discount-only or MFN-only investor holding this letter would compute a pro rata share of zero.
- **Conditions / termination.** Terminates on the earlier of (i) the initial closing of the Equity Financing, (ii) immediately prior to the closing of a Liquidity Event, or (iii) immediately prior to the Dissolution Event. Note (i): the right dies at the closing it exists to let the Investor participate in.
- **Amendment and assignment.** Amendable by the Company plus either the Investor or `the holders of a majority of shares of Capital Stock issued from all Safes converted in connection with the Equity Financing held by the Investor and other Safe holders with Pro Rata Rights`, with equal treatment required and prompt notice to a non-consenting Investor. Assignable only to affiliates without Company consent. Governing law is imported: `consistent with that set forth in the Investor’s Safe`.

### Conclusion

Invariant across all six SAFEs: the §1(c) Dissolution Event mechanic, the §1(d) liquidation-priority
ladder, the §1(e) termination triggers, the definitions of Dissolution Event, Dividend Amount,
Equity Financing, Proceeds and Safe, and §3(a)–(e) apart from the organisation line,
the constitutional-document line and the added `security interest, encumbrance or` in §3(c). The §5 miscellaneous clauses on amendment, notice,
non-voting status, assignment and severability are invariant in substance everywhere.

What varies by **economic term only** is a tight cluster: the cover term line, the §1(a) and §1(b)
payout formulas, one or two conversion-price definitions, and — for the cap variant alone — six
supporting definitions that exist purely to make a capitalization denominator computable. Removing
the cap removes a quarter of the document; adding MFN adds one definition, one new section and a
10-day deadline.

What varies by **jurisdiction only** is the wrapper, not the maths. The two priced formulas
(`Safe Price` and `Liquidity Price`) and both capitalization bullet lists survive verbatim into
Singapore, Canada and Cayman. What genuinely changes is the _event_ vocabulary — Change of Control,
Direct Listing and Initial Public Offering are all redefined on substance, not just wording — plus
legends, securities-law representations, forum selection and currency.

**Do the axes interact?** Not observably, because YC ships only the cap-only form outside the US, so
five of the twelve (term × jurisdiction) cells do not exist. The one inference worth recording: the
Pro Rata Agreement's numerator is defined over Safes bearing `a “Post-Money Valuation Cap”`, and it
is published in all four jurisdictions, so the side-letter formula is well-defined in exactly the
cells YC actually ships and would evaluate to zero in the unshipped US-discount and US-MFN cells.
Section 5(a)'s majority-in-interest grouping is the only place where the two axes are named
together, and it is jurisdiction-invariant: every one of the six documents groups Safes by
`“Post-Money Valuation Cap”` **and** `“Discount Rate”`, including the three that define neither or
only one of those terms.

## E. Surprises

1. **Canada's SAFE cites an undefined term, twice, in one sentence.** L101: `“**Safe** **Preferred Shares**” means the shares of the series of Preferred **Stock** issued to the Investor … as the shares of Standard Preferred **Stock**`. These are the _only_ two occurrences of the string `Stock` in any of the three jurisdiction SAFEs (`grep -c` confirms 0 in SG and KY). The Canadian form defines `Preferred Shares` and `Standard Preferred Shares` and never defines `Preferred Stock`. The definition of the class the Investor actually receives points at nothing.

2. **Cayman's side letter and Cayman's SAFE disagree on the class name.** `Pro Rata Side Letter (Cayman).md` L7 grants a right over `Standard Preferred Shares`; the Cayman SAFE §2 defines only `Standard Preference Shares`. The Singapore pair is internally consistent (`Preference` in both). Cayman appears to have been produced from the Canadian side letter rather than its own SAFE.

3. **The MFN §1(d) contradicts itself.** Because E8 substitutes `Cash-Out Amount` for `Conversion Amount` throughout, §1(d) first says the Cash-Out Amount is `(iii) Senior to payments for Common Stock`, then says in the next paragraph that `The Investor’s right to receive its Cash-Out Amount is (A) on par with payments for Common Stock`. In the cap and discount forms these two paragraphs govern two different amounts, so there is no clash. The MFN form collapsed them without deleting the second.

4. **MFN defines `Liquidity Price` and never uses it operatively.** Its only appearance outside its own definition is inside `Dividend Amount` (L47). §1(b) pays the Purchase Amount flat. So an MFN Safe carries a full fair-market-value pricing definition that fires only if the Company declares a cash dividend.

5. **Singapore quietly drops the Company's void-and-return remedy.** The US, Canadian and Cayman §4(b) all say the Company `may void this Safe and return the Purchase Amount` if the Investor is not accredited at the Equity Financing. Singapore's §4(b) has no such sentence. This is not a vocabulary consequence of switching from Rule 501 to the SFA — the remedy is a contract term, and it simply is not there.

6. **The Cayman securities legend is a corrupted search-and-replace.** L3 reads `may not be offered, sold or otherwise transferred, SUBJECT TO SECURITY or hypothecated` — `PLEDGED` was replaced by `SUBJECT TO SECURITY`, producing a list whose third item is a phrase and not a verb. The surrounding capitalisation is scrambled (`UNITED STATES OF AMERICA` and `ANY OTHER JURISDICTION` in caps inside an otherwise sentence-case bolded paragraph). This is the only legend in the family that is not clean.

7. **Canada localises the securities law but not the corporate law.** J27: Singapore says `constitution`, Cayman says `memorandum and articles of association`, and Canada still says `certificate of incorporation or bylaws` — US terminology, in a document that added a Canadian hold legend, an Ontario private-issuer representation and a Canadian-regulator disclosure consent.

8. **Singapore can put the Investor into an SPV without asking it.** §4(c) (J31) lets the board plus a majority-in-interest of _other_ Safe holders elect to satisfy the conversion by issuing the Investor shares in a special purpose vehicle instead of shares in the Company. The Investor's own consent is not among the two elections required. No other jurisdiction has this.

9. **The US form has no forum-selection clause.** It picks a governing law and stops. All three jurisdiction forms add `submits to the non-exclusive jurisdiction of the Courts of …`, and all three drop the US form's `without regard to the conflicts of law provisions of such jurisdiction`.

10. **Singapore's governing-law clause has a stray article:** `the laws of **the** Singapore` (L141).

11. **The Pro Rata Right terminates at the moment it becomes exercisable.** Clause (i) of the termination trigger is `the initial closing of the Equity Financing` — the same closing at which the Standard Preferred Stock it entitles the holder to buy is sold. Identical in all four side letters, so it is not a localisation slip.

12. **Singapore's side letter says `a majority of shares of Capital Shares`** (¶4). Canada and Cayman deleted the `shares of` periphrasis at that spot; Singapore substituted the noun and left the periphrasis standing. Harmless, but it is the fingerprint of a per-file hand edit rather than one shared localisation pass — consistent with finding 2, where Cayman's side letter tracks Canada's and not its own SAFE.
