SG Child Support Package

Every figure on this page was computed by an executable L4 encoding of the scheme and the schemes it replaces. Nothing here is worked out in JavaScript.

This is an announcement, not law. The Package was announced at the National Day Rally on 23 August 2026 and is to be implemented from 1 April 2027. No Bill has been introduced and the Child Development Co-Savings Act 2001 is unamended. Every figure dated on or after 1 April 2027 states a policy intention. Not legal advice.

1 — What one child gets

The Package is stated as “up to $62,000 for every Singapore Citizen child”. That figure is reached by exactly one cohort. Pick a child and the encoding says what they actually get.

Against the schemes being replaced

2 — The cohort table

The five Package lines, by year of birth. Support is flat across birth orders in every cohort but one — which is the scheme doing what it says.

3 — The line that goes down

The $10,000 Baby Gift replaces a Baby Bonus Cash Gift of $11,000, or $13,000 for a third or subsequent child. It is a floor, so nothing is recovered — but the instalments stop, and the cash line falls for every child born from the Budget 2023 enhancement onward.

4 — A deadline worth acting on

Government co-matching into a Child Development Account is capped by birth order today: $4,000, $7,000, $9,000 and $15,000. On 1 October 2027 every cap becomes $5,000. Headroom not used by 30 September 2027 is gone.

Two readings of “all caps adjust to $5,000” survive the announcement, and this page shows the one the encoding takes: the $5,000 is a lifetime total. On the rival reading it is fresh headroom and every child gets a further $5,000. Both execute in the encoding; fork F1 records why.

5 — Childcare leave, and who pays for it

Today the Act gives 2 to 6 days, graduated by months served, and caps the combined entitlement at six days however many children you have. The announced scheme gives 8, 10 or 12 days by number of children aged 12 and below. The Government says it will cover the cost “up to the reimbursement limit” — reported unchanged at $500 a day.

Above a gross daily rate of about $800 with one child — about $667 with three — the employer’s bill rises, because the day count roughly doubles while the reimbursement limit does not move.

6 — What it costs the budget

Per-child entitlements come from the encoding. The population figures are yours to set: this is the multiplication an agency would run, and the reason a scheme like this is worth encoding at all.

A steady-state annual cost, not a cash-flow forecast: it prices one birth cohort's whole entitlement from birth to 17, which in steady state is what the scheme costs each year once every cohort is in the system. Disbursements are spread over eighteen years and the transition years cost more, because existing cohorts are being brought in at once.
